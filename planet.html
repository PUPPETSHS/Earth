Third planet from the Sun

What planetEarthlooks like

From space, Earth looks like a blue and white ball with brown and green patches. A thin glowing edge of air wraps around it. About seven tenths of the surface is ocean, and clouds cover roughly two thirds of the planet at any moment.

Five distances

The view changes as you move away

Earth never looks the same twice. Here is what it looks like from five real places, nearest first.

400 kmSpace station

A curved horizon and a thin blue line

Earth fills the window and the horizon bends. A narrow blue and white band hugs the edge. That band is the whole atmosphere seen sideways. The station circles the planet about every 90 minutes, so the view switches between day and night roughly every 45 minutes.

35,786 kmGeostationary orbit

One half of the planet, held still

A whole hemisphere sits in view as a round disc. Satellites at this height circle at the same rate Earth turns, so they stay above one spot. Weather satellites live here and watch clouds spiral across the same half of the planet all day.

384,400 kmThe Moon

A blue disc with phases

Earth looks about four times wider than the Moon looks from here. It hangs in nearly the same spot in the lunar sky and goes through phases like a moon of its own. The Apollo 8 crew saw the first Earthrise in December 1968.

1.5 million kmSun-Earth L1

The full sunlit face

A satellite parked on the sunward side sees the lit half of Earth head on, like a full moon. As the day passes, the continents slide across the disc one after another.

6 billion kmVoyager 1, 1990

Less than one pixel

Seen from the edge of the planets, Earth is a faint blue speck inside a band of scattered sunlight. The picture is known as the Pale Blue Dot. Everything described on this page fits inside that single point.

Reading the colors

What each color is

71% ocean29% land

Deep blue

Open ocean. Water absorbs red light and scatters blue, so deep water looks dark blue and shallow water near coasts looks lighter.

White

Clouds and ice. The polar caps and the Greenland ice sheet stay white all year, while clouds drift and swirl.

Green, brown and tan

Land. Forests and grassland look green. Deserts such as the Sahara and inland Australia look tan or orange.

Black with gold dots

The night side. City lights glow in clusters along coasts and rivers. The big dark gaps are oceans, deserts and ice.

99%

The glowing edge is very thin

About 99% of the air sits in the lowest 30 km, while the planet is 6,371 km from surface to center. On a basketball, the atmosphere would be about half a millimetre thick.
